About the Workshop

The Data Agents over Heterogeneous and Evolving Data workshop establishes insight discovery over evolving, multi-source data as a focused research agenda at the intersection of machine learning, natural language processing, data systems, human-computer interaction, and AI for science.

Unlike workshops centered on table representation, query generation, generic agents, or domain-specific applications, we center the full insight-discovery lifecycle: evidence acquisition, reasoning, tool use, verification, memory, adaptation, and decision support.

The workshop brings together researchers and practitioners working on data-centric reasoning, retrieval, memory, verification, evaluation, human-agent collaboration, and trustworthy AI systems.

We organize the workshop around three primary questions:

  1. How can data agents discover, align, and integrate evidence across structured, unstructured, and multimodal sources that are fragmented, inconsistent, and partially observed?
  2. How can data agents reason over evolving data, while maintaining memory of schemas, assumptions, and prior findings, and adapting as distributions drift, semantics shift, and user goals change, without silently invalidating earlier conclusions?
  3. How can data agents ground insights in provenance and recomputable evidence, quantify uncertainty, proactively surface emerging patterns, and support human decision-making so that their outputs are trustworthy enough to act on?

These questions map to five themes in our call for papers and program: (i) evidence discovery and cross-source alignment; (ii) reasoning and tool use for insight discovery; (iii) memory, adaptation, and self-evolution; (iv) grounding, verification, and trustworthiness; and (v) benchmarks and evaluation.

Call for Papers

Topics of Interest

We welcome submissions across the full journey from siloed data sources to trustworthy, actionable insights, including but not limited to:

  • Evidence discovery and cross-source alignment

    • Discovering relevant evidence across structured, unstructured, and multimodal sources.
    • Schema linking, entity resolution, semantic retrieval, and concept alignment across fragmented data ecosystems.
    • Automatic inference of semantic and structural relationships without hand-engineered schemas.

  • Reasoning and tool use for insight discovery

    • Multi-step reasoning and planning for analytical tasks over heterogeneous, partially observed data.
    • Tool use, code generation, and execution grounding for querying, cleaning, analysis, visualization, and experiment design.
    • Proactive discovery of patterns, anomalies, hypotheses, and opportunities in evolving data.

  • Memory, adaptation, and self-evolution

    • Memory over schemas, metrics, domain semantics, prior analyses, assumptions, and findings.
    • Adaptation to schema/metric drift, distribution shift, evolving semantics, and changing goals.
    • Self-improvement from traces, feedback, workflows, and accumulated analytical skills.

  • Grounding, verification, and trustworthiness

    • Grounding analytical findings in provenance, lineage, and retrieved sources.
    • Verification of reasoning chains, generated code, SQL, charts, and statistical claims.
    • Uncertainty quantification, conflict detection, and the controllability, privacy, fairness, and auditability of autonomous data agents.

  • Benchmarks and Evaluation

    • Benchmarks for end-to-end insight discovery across domains, modalities, and evolving data.
    • Metrics for insight quality, novelty, actionability, verifiability, and decision usefulness.
    • Human-agent protocols for clarification, validation, feedback, and workflow refinement.

Submission Details

We invite submissions up to 9 pages following NeurIPS workshop formatting guidelines. Reviews will be double-blind via OpenReview. Workshop publications are non-archival; accepted papers will be featured on the workshop website and presented as posters.
